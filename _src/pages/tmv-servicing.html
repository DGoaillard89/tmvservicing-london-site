A thermostatic mixing valve blends hot and cold water to a safe temperature before it reaches the tap, shower or bath. Left alone, it slowly drifts, scales up and gets lazy. Then one day the cold supply drops out and it doesn't shut off like it should. Servicing is how you stop that day happening.

What happens on a TMV service

Every valve gets the same routine, whether it's the only one in a Fulham conversion or one of four hundred in a Lewisham care home.

  1. Find it and record it. Location, make, model, type (TMV2 or TMV3) and the outlet it feeds. If you don't have an asset register yet, this is where it starts.
  2. Check the supplies. We measure the hot and cold coming in. A TMV can only mix what it's given, so a lukewarm hot supply or a warm cold supply gets flagged, because that's a wider Legionella control issue too.
  3. Measure the mixed temperature at the outlet and compare it with the set point for that outlet type.
  4. Fail-safe test. We isolate the cold supply and watch what the valve does. It should shut off, or drop to a trickle, quickly. Then we put it back and check it recovers properly.
  5. Strip and clean. Strainers out, check valves out, scale and debris off. In London that usually means a proper descale, not a quick rinse.
  6. Cartridge and seals. We descale the thermostatic cartridge or replace it if it's past saving, and swap tired seals.
  7. Recalibrate and retest. Set the outlet temperature, repeat the fail-safe test and record the final readings.
  8. Write it up. A certificate for each valve, the logbook updated, and a short list of anything that needs more than a service.

Hard water is the London problem

London's water is hard. You'll know this if you've ever looked inside a kettle in Clapham. Inside a TMV, the same scale coats the thermostatic element so it reacts slower, packs the strainers so flow drops, and stops check valves seating properly so hot and cold can cross over.

That's why descaling is part of every service we do here, and why we'll be honest when your interval needs to come down. A valve in a soft-water town might happily go a year. The same valve in a busy London shower block might need looking at every six months.

What we'll tell you: how scaled each valve was, whether the strainers were restricting flow, and whether we think your current service interval is right. If it isn't, we'll say so, with the readings to back it up.

How often should TMVs be serviced?

HTM 04-01, the Department of Health guidance for healthcare water systems, sets out the approach most people follow in other buildings too. A newly commissioned valve gets in-service tests at around 6 to 8 weeks and again at 12 to 15 weeks. After that, the interval depends on what the tests show.

  • If the mixed temperature has barely moved, the interval can stretch out.
  • If it has drifted noticeably, the valve gets serviced and the interval stays short.

The D 08 supplement to HTM 04-01 (Annex F) gives the starting point: a valve that holds its temperature within about 1°C can wait 24 to 28 weeks for its next test, and one that drifts more gets tested again sooner. HTM 04-01 Part B also lists TMV strainer servicing annually, or as your risk assessment directs. London's hard water tends to make valves drift faster, so your own test history is the best guide. Your Legionella risk assessment and written scheme should say what your site needs, and we'll work to that. Our TMV compliance guide goes into the detail.

Common questions

How long does a TMV service take?

Usually 20 to 40 minutes per valve once we can get at it. Heavily scaled valves and ones hidden behind tiled-in panels take longer. Access is normally the bigger factor in a London building.

Will the water be off while you work?

Only at the outlet we're working on, and only for the time it takes. We isolate locally wherever there are isolation valves. If there aren't any, we'll talk to you before we touch anything bigger.

What if a valve fails the fail-safe test?

We'll try to bring it back with a clean, descale and new parts. If it still won't shut off properly, we'll tell you there and then, make the outlet safe if needed, and give you a price to replace it.

Do you service all makes of TMV?

Yes, we service the common makes found in UK buildings. Some older or discontinued valves can't get parts any more, and then replacement is usually the sensible answer.

Do you record readings to the D 08 procedure?

Yes. For each valve we record the mixed temperature at full flow, the result of the cold isolation test and the temperature once the cold is restored, following clause 11.2 of the D 08 supplement. We also note whether the strainers and check valves were clean. Those readings are what lets you set sensible intervals later on.

Can you service valves in occupied flats or care home bedrooms?

Yes, that's where most of them are. We agree a room order with you, knock and introduce ourselves, and keep each visit short. If a resident isn't in or would rather we came back, we note it and book a return so no valve gets quietly skipped.

What happens to rooms you couldn't get into?

They're listed on your report as not accessed, with the reason, and they stay flagged as due on your asset register. We'll offer return dates straight away. A missed valve that nobody chases is a common gap auditors pick up on, so we don't let them drop off the list.
