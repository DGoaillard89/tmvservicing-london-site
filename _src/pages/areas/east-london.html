East London has seen a huge amount of new building over the last couple of decades. Canary Wharf, Stratford and the Royal Docks have filled with towers, student blocks and serviced apartments, while Hackney and Bethnal Green keep their terraces and estates. Further out, Redbridge, Barking & Dagenham and Havering are more suburban. All of it has TMVs, and in newer blocks they're easy to forget about once the developer hands over.

The buildings we see in East London

New-build towers and build-to-rent

Around Canary Wharf, Stratford, the Isle of Dogs and the Royal Docks, high-rise blocks often have TMVs on every bath and sometimes on basins and showers too, hidden behind magnetic or screw-fix panels. Many were fitted at construction and quietly forgotten. Build-to-rent operators and managing agents increasingly want a proper register and service programme, and that's where we come in.

Purpose-built student accommodation

Student blocks around Mile End, Stratford and Whitechapel have hundreds of near-identical en-suites. Summer, between academic years, is the natural window for servicing when rooms are empty, though summer lets and conference guests can complicate that. We plan by floor so cleaners and maintenance teams can follow behind.

Council estates and housing associations

Tower Hamlets, Hackney, Newham and Barking & Dagenham have large social housing portfolios, from post-war estates to new mixed-tenure schemes. Sheltered and supported housing blocks often have a TMV on every outlet. With social landlords under more scrutiny on home safety, a clear service record matters.

Serviced apartments, hotels and offices

Docklands hotels and aparthotels and the Canary Wharf offices all run busy showers and washrooms. Early starts and weekend work are common requests here.

Suburban homes, schools and care

In Redbridge and Havering we see more care homes, schools and family houses converted to HMOs. Much easier parking, much longer drives between jobs.

What we typically find

  • Valves no one knew about. New-build flats where residents (and sometimes managing agents) don't know the TMV exists until we find it behind the bath panel.
  • Boosted supplies in towers. High-rise water systems are pumped, so supply pressures vary floor by floor. We check that the valve suits the pressures it's actually getting.
  • Hard water. East London is supplied from the Thames and Lee catchments and the water is hard. Scale build-up is typical on valves that haven't been serviced since handover.

Getting around

Docklands developments often have private estate roads, concierge desks and strict loading bay booking, so we book in advance. Hackney and Tower Hamlets have extensive CPZs. Further east, the A13 and A12 are the main routes. We group jobs so one engineer can cover a cluster of blocks in a day rather than criss-crossing the patch.
