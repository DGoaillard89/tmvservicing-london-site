South London is our biggest patch: Lambeth, Southwark, Wandsworth, Lewisham, Greenwich, Bexley, Bromley, Croydon, Merton, Sutton and Kingston upon Thames. Inner boroughs like Southwark and Lambeth are dense and urban, while Bromley and Sutton feel almost like the Home Counties. The building stock changes a lot as you go south, and so does the way we plan the work.

The buildings we see in South London

Big council estates and housing associations

Southwark and Lambeth have some of the largest council housing portfolios in London, and housing associations manage thousands more homes across Lewisham, Greenwich and Croydon. Sheltered housing, extra-care schemes and supported living are common, and they tend to have TMVs on every bath and basin. We plan estate-wide programmes block by block, with resident letters and booked return visits for no-access homes.

Purpose-built flats and riverside developments

Nine Elms, Battersea, Bankside, Greenwich Peninsula and Deptford have filled with new apartment blocks. As in East London, the TMVs fitted at construction are often forgotten about until something goes wrong or a managing agent starts asking questions.

Care homes in the suburbs

Croydon, Bromley, Sutton and Kingston have a large number of care and nursing homes. Many are in extended Victorian or Edwardian villas, where the plumbing has grown in stages and TMVs can turn up in unexpected places. TMV3 valves, a tidy register and records that are easy to show an inspector are what these homes need.

Schools, colleges and leisure centres

The southern boroughs have large school estates and plenty of council and trust-run leisure centres. Changing rooms and showers in leisure centres see relentless use, which makes scale build-up and drift more likely. We service them early in the morning or in quieter spells.

Victorian terraces and HMOs

Brixton, Peckham, Tooting, Balham, Lewisham and New Cross are full of converted terraces, many let as HMOs or flats. The same story as elsewhere: shared bathrooms, mixed valve types and a licence to keep happy.

South London water

Thames Water supplies most of South London, partly from the Thames and partly from chalk boreholes, and either way it's hard. SES Water supplies much of Sutton and some neighbouring areas, and a few places near the Surrey and Kent borders are on other suppliers, so your bill is the quickest way to check. Expect to hear about scaled strainers. Thames Water's postcode checker or your own supplier's will give you the hardness at your address.

What we typically find

  • Long estate programmes with access gaps. A first visit rarely reaches every flat. We track no-access homes and keep going back until the list is done, or until you tell us otherwise.
  • Drift in busy showers. Leisure centre and gym valves that get used hundreds of times a day tend to drift faster, so their test intervals often end up shorter.
  • Older valves needing replacement. Valves fitted in 1990s refurbishments are often past the point where parts are available. We'll give you a priced plan rather than a surprise.

Getting around

Inner South London has extensive CPZs, and some estates have their own parking rules and permits. Further out, parking is easier but distances are bigger: Bexley to Kingston is a long way on the South Circular. We split the patch into inner and outer rounds so engineers aren't spending half the day in traffic. Planning around CPZs and access explains how.

TMV servicing borough by borough

Eleven boroughs is a lot of ground. Here's what we tend to find in each.

Lambeth

{{pc:Lambeth}}

From Waterloo and Vauxhall down through Brixton, Clapham and Streatham to West Norwood. Large council estates, Victorian terraces split into flats and HMOs, offices near the river and St Thomas' Hospital. Controlled parking covers most of the north of the borough.

Southwark

{{pc:Southwark}}

Offices and new flats around Bankside and London Bridge, Guy's Hospital, big estates and regeneration schemes around Elephant and Castle and Peckham, then quieter Victorian streets towards Dulwich. Inner Southwark parking needs planning; the south of the borough is easier.

Wandsworth

{{pc:Wandsworth}}

The Nine Elms and Battersea riverside has filled with new apartment towers, while Clapham Junction, Balham and Tooting are mostly Victorian terraces. Add St George's Hospital in Tooting and the large post-war Alton Estate in Roehampton, and it's quite a mix.

Lewisham

{{pc:Lewisham}}

Victorian and Edwardian terraces in Brockley, Catford and Forest Hill, newer blocks around Deptford and Lewisham town centre, and Goldsmiths' student accommodation in New Cross. Lots of flats and shared houses, plus schools and sheltered housing.

Greenwich

{{pc:Greenwich}}

Big new developments on Greenwich Peninsula and at Woolwich's Royal Arsenal, older housing in Charlton and Eltham, and part of Thamesmead. Hospital, university and school sites as well, so a real spread of TMV types.

Bexley

{{pc:Bexley}}

Largely suburban, with inter-war and post-war houses around Bexleyheath, Sidcup and Welling, plus care homes, schools and part of Thamesmead. Parking is usually simple; the journey from W4 is the long bit, so we group Bexley visits together.

Bromley

{{pc:Bromley}}

London's largest borough by area, much of it green belt. Beckenham, Bromley and Orpington have plenty of care homes, schools and purpose-built flats. Easy parking, long distances.

Croydon

{{pc:Croydon}}

Croydon town centre has seen a lot of office blocks converted into flats, alongside new towers, while Purley, Coulsdon and Thornton Heath are more suburban, with many care homes and supported housing schemes. The A23 and the town-centre one-way roads take some planning.

Merton

{{pc:Merton}}

Victorian terraces and flats in Wimbledon and Colliers Wood, inter-war housing in Morden and Mitcham, and schools and care homes throughout. We steer clear of the streets around the All England Club during the Wimbledon fortnight if we can.

Sutton

{{pc:Sutton}}

Suburban Sutton, Cheam, Carshalton and Wallington, including part of the inter-war St Helier estate. Care homes, schools and family houses make up most of the work. Much of the borough is supplied by SES Water rather than Thames Water, but the water is still hard.

Kingston upon Thames

{{pc:Kingston upon Thames}}

Kingston town centre has Kingston University's halls and newer flats, and Surbiton has big Victorian villas, many split into flats. Further south, Tolworth and Chessington are suburban. The A3 and Kingston's one-way system shape how we plan the day.

Richmond upon Thames straddles the river and we plan it with West London. Just over the bridges, see Central London.

South London questions

Do you really cover all eleven South London boroughs?

Yes, all eleven: Lambeth, Southwark, Wandsworth, Lewisham, Greenwich, Bexley, Bromley, Croydon, Merton, Sutton and Kingston upon Thames. We're based in Chiswick and plan the patch as inner and outer rounds, so the further-out boroughs like Bexley and Bromley get grouped visits rather than one-off trips across town.

Who supplies water in South London, and is it hard?

Thames Water supplies most of South London and SES Water supplies much of Sutton and some neighbouring areas. The water is hard across the patch, which is why every service we do includes cleaning strainers and descaling. Your supplier's postcode checker gives the hardness for your address.

Can you run an estate-wide TMV programme for a South London housing provider?

Yes. We work block by block, send letters to residents, keep a register of every valve and keep track of homes we couldn't get into until each one is done. With Southwark and Lambeth's large estates, that tracking is usually the hardest part. Our post on housing associations and hot water safety covers the background.

Can you service leisure centre and gym showers without closing them?

Usually, yes. We work early in the morning or in quieter spells, a bank of showers at a time, so the changing rooms stay open. Busy shower valves tend to drift faster, so we'll suggest intervals based on what the readings show. Our post on leisure centre and gym showers has more.

Services in South London

Every service we offer is available south of the river: TMV servicing and testing, installation and replacement and asset registers and scheduling. Care homes in Croydon, Bromley or Sutton may find our post on TMV3 valves and CQC useful.
